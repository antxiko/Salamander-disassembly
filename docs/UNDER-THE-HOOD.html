Under the hood

Getting started

This repository does not include the game. It includes the tools that, from your own image of the cartridge, rebuild the commented listing, reassemble it and check that the ROM comes out byte for byte.

What you need

  • The image of Salamander - Operation X (RC-758), 131,072 bytes, in the root as salamander.rom. Its sha256: edbbba820ac67d6a55b9dc5da9309e45979936d5620c07d09793efaf077e86b9.
  • make, python3 and the pasmo assembler.
  • For the pictures and the music, Python with PIL, numpy and lameenc. For stage 7, also the Nemesis 2 image at ../NEMESIS2_DISAM/nemesis2.rom.

What to run

make               # traces, builds the listing, reassembles and runs the tests
make verify        # the sixteen banks against the ROM
make densidad      # how many instructions carry a comment
make sanity        # the budget: not one byte unassigned

make verify has to say OK: la ROM entera reproducible byte a byte.

Where everything is

  • src/salamander_pNN.asm: the listing of each bank (p00 to p15).
  • src/pNN.notes: the names, comments and data blocks of each bank. The listing is generated from here.
  • tools/vram.py, tools/mundo.py, tools/nave.py, tools/enemigos.py, tools/jefes.py, tools/repite.py: the pictures.
  • tools/musica.py: the music; tools/textos.py: the texts.
  • tools/omsx_vram.tcl and tools/omsx_psg.tcl: the openMSX probes everything is checked against.

The pictures

python tools/imagenes.py docs/imagenes

The routes, the ship and the characters come from the ROM alone. The enemies and the bosses are drawn with the ROM's patterns over what they do in openMSX. The screens of the intro, the planet choice and the endings are rebuilt by repeating, with the ROM's bytes, the cartridge's writes to the VRAM, and each one is identical, pixel for pixel, to the emulator's VRAM at that moment. The gauge pieces and the items come from the ROM (tools/armas.py, which checks them against the VRAM). The animations are the VRAM and VDP registers of every frame of a game in openMSX (VRAM_CUADROS in tools/omsx_vram.tcl), painted the way the TMS9918 paints them (tools/anima.py).

The cartridge

The machine

A first generation MSX: the TMS9918 VDP in SCREEN 2, the PSG and the SCC carried by the cartridge itself. Names at 0x3800, colours at 0x0000, patterns at 0x2000, sprite patterns at 0x1800 and their attributes at 0x3B00. The play area is 21 rows; the three below are the status panel.

The mapper

Konami's with SCC: sixteen 8 KB banks. Bank 0 is fixed at 0x4000 and the other three slots are chosen at 0x7000, 0x9000 and 0xB000, with a copy at 0xF0F1-0xF0F3. The game sets them three at a time (p00:416E-p00:417D): 1-2-3 for the game, 4-5-6 for the sound, 7-8-9 for the graphics and 13-14-15 for the maps. Bank 10 goes alone at 0x8000 (the intro, the cut-scenes and the endings) and banks 11 and 12 alone at 0xA000 (the bosses and the special enemies). Bank 0x3F at 0x8000 opens the SCC.

The interrupt

INIT (p00:40DA) clears the game RAM, installs the H.KEYI hook and stays in jr $: everything runs inside the interrupt (p00:4069). First the sound, with banks 4-5-6 (p04:6978); then, if the previous frame has finished (0xE205), a game frame.

The sound

Eight channels of 0x2C bytes from 0xE000: three for the PSG and five for the SCC. There are 89 sounds; each has a header with its channels and its priority (p04:663E) and one score per channel. Effects use two channels (PSG C and the first SCC one) and tunes six or eight, so a six-channel tune keeps playing under the effects. Orders 0xF0-0xF6 handle the pause, the fades and moving the SCC channels to the PSG (p04:6472).

The RAM

The game uses 0xE000-0xEFFF. The 12 enemies are 0x40-byte records at 0xE700, the ship's 15 shots are at 0xE440 and the boss at 0xEC80. The screen is built in RAM, at 0xCA00 with 64 bytes per row, and every frame it is dumped to the VRAM (p00:5FED).

The other cartridge

At start-up p03:B599 looks at the other slot. If it finds Nemesis 2 (p03:B5E3 compares its 0x4010 with CD 07 51 and six bytes of its 0x7FFA), stage 7 opens up. To draw it, p00:5B91 copies to 0xC000 a routine that maps the other cartridge in and brings 1,137 bytes, twenty pieces of its banks 7, 8 and 9 (table p00:5BFE), to 0xC100.

The code

The numbers

100 % of the 131,072 bytes is explained: 52,996 of code and 78,076 of data. The nine banks with code have 3,429 routines and labels, and 11,342 of their 25,417 instructions carry a comment: 44.6 %, with no routine under 10 %.

Where everything is

  • bank 0: the interrupt, the bank sets, the graphics loading (p00:438C), the maps in RAM (p00:49BB, p00:4A88) and the game states;
  • banks 1, 2 and 3: the enemies, the ship, its weapons, the collisions and the script of each stage (p02:8525-p02:86EF);
  • bank 4: the sound;
  • bank 10: the intro, the take-off, the planet choice, the cut-scenes and the endings;
  • bank 11: the eight bosses (table p01:6E89) and the screen fade;
  • bank 12: the special enemies, the items to pick up and the SELECTION screen;
  • banks 5, 7, 8, 9, 13, 14 and 15: data only (waveforms, graphics, maps and texts).

The names

The routine and RAM names are in tools/anotaciones.py, and tools/anota.py takes them to every call and every access in the listing.

Salamander - Operation X was published by Konami for the MSX in 1987; its catalogue number is RC-758 and it is 128 KB. All rights in the game remain with their holders. This is preservation, study and documentation work, and the cartridge image is not distributed.
